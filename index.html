Every format needed a reason to exist.

Object reels made positioning visual. Agency-life humour was designed for shares. The founder series built familiarity. Rapid-fire questions created a batchable format. Sunday carousels carried the educational and qualifying role.

Content direction, a four-month calendar, hooks, concepts, shooting and editing. I shot and edited more than ten reels across this account and ATlog myself.

Help them spot the gap. Show them the next step.

The educational carousels helped founders assess their brand readiness, then pointed them towards a free brand audit. The content gave them a useful way to think about the problem before inviting them into a sales conversation.

CASE 02 / ATLOG

Nobody learns an app
from one post.

ATlog is a location-based social app in beta, built in-house by the founder of Jagroots. It had three check-in modes, no audience and no visual identity on Instagram, and I had two months to build the communication around it.

A / THE THINKING

Twenty posts, planned as one picture.

Was@, Am@ and WillBe@ each got their own moment, released one per fortnight rather than crowded into a single explainer. The grid was planned as one composition, with the mascot running down the centre column so the profile had a rhythm before it had an audience. The positioning line — Made at India, not Made in India — was already set when I joined. My job was to build twenty posts that taught it.

B / MY CONTRIBUTION

From positioning to production.

Content direction, grid plan, calendar, reel scripts, launch copy and creative planning. The remaining scripted reels were shot, edited and approved into the client’s content repository; publication was later paused on the client side.

C / WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS

Twice the views. That’s all it proves.

The three-reel launch campaign got roughly twice the views of anything previously on the account. That’s a view comparison — not downloads, not sign-ups, not business growth.

02 / HOW I THINK

I start with the problem,
not the post.

01

Look closer.

Understand the brand, the audience, the category and the actual problem. Public comments and competitor activity are clues—not proof.

02

Find the point.

Decide what the audience needs to hear, why it should matter to them and what makes the idea fit this particular brand.

03

Make it real.

Turn the thinking into words, formats, campaigns, content systems or practical deliverables. Good strategy has to survive execution.

04

See what happened.

Look at the available evidence, ask what worked and what didn't, and use it to decide what to test or improve next.

I don't think a clever idea automatically makes good marketing. It still has to fit the audience, the brand and the job.

03 / THE PERSON BEHIND THE TABS

Marketing brain.
Story-shaped heart.

Pune, India · books, films and far too much coffee

I'm Ayushi—a marketer who likes the space where a good observation becomes a useful idea.

I studied finance before moving into marketing, which probably explains why I don't want creativity and measurement treated as opposing forces. I like the human side of a brand: what people notice, what they ignore, what makes them trust something and what makes them remember it.

I'm drawn to writing, pop culture, films, books, everyday absurdities and the small details that can become a line, a hook or a bigger idea. I'm still sharpening the craft, especially the art of making a thought land in fewer words.

I learn by doing, ask questions before jumping into execution and work best when the brief is clear and there's room to think. I like collaboration, useful feedback and being part of the reasoning—not just the hand-off.
