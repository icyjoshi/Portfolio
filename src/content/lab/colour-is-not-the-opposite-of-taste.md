There is a particular look that has taken over a certain kind of brand:
off-white background, one thin sans-serif, a photograph with the
saturation pulled down, and a great deal of space. It reads as expensive.
It also reads as every other brand doing the same thing, which is the
part nobody mentions.

Minimalism became shorthand for taste. And it is a genuinely good
default, in the way that a plain white shirt is a good default — right
often enough that choosing it stops being a decision.

## The actual argument against colour

Not "colour is unsophisticated." The real objection is that colour is
harder. A restrained palette is very forgiving; a bold one exposes every
weak decision underneath it. If the hierarchy is muddled, beige hides it
and pink does not.

So the retreat to neutral is often not a taste decision. It is a risk
decision, made by people who would rather be invisible than wrong.

## What I think is actually going on

People made colourful, characterful work for decades without any tooling
help at all. It is genuinely strange that the moment it became cheap to
produce variations, so much output went flatter rather than braver.

The constraint was never the tools. It was that colour requires you to
have decided something — who this is for, what it should feel like, what
you are willing to be disliked for. Neutral lets you skip that.

## The version I would defend

Colour with a job. Not fifteen colours competing; two or three, each
doing something specific. One for personality, one for the thing you want
clicked, one dark enough to carry the serious moments.

That is a system, and a system is what stops "colourful" from becoming
"loud".

The failure mode of minimalism is that nobody remembers you. The failure
mode of colour is that some people do not like you. Given a choice, I
know which risk I would rather take.
