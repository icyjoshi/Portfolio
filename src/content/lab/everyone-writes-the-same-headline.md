Do keyword research for almost any B2B term and something slightly
depressing happens. You open the top ten results and they are the same
article. Same structure, same subheadings, often the same three points in
the same order.

This is not laziness. It is what happens when everybody optimises against
the same signals. You look at what is ranking, you match it, you add
slightly more of it. Repeat across a category for a few years and the
whole first page converges.

## Why parity is a losing position

The logic behind "match what is ranking" assumes the reader is choosing
between you and nothing. They are not. They are choosing between you and
nine near-identical alternatives, and if you have successfully matched
all nine, you have removed every reason to pick you specifically.

Parity is not a ranking strategy. It is a tie, and ties get broken by
domain authority — which, if you are the new one, you do not have.

## The more useful move

Two things I keep coming back to:

**Go narrower.** The terms with realistic difficulty are almost always
more specific and more awkwardly phrased than the category term everyone
is fighting over. Smaller volume, and considerably better intent —
somebody typing a long, specific, slightly weird query is further along
in the decision than somebody typing the category name.

**Say the thing the other nine will not.** Usually there is one honest
sentence the whole category avoids, because it complicates the sale. The
piece that says it out loud is the one people forward.

Neither is clever. Both are harder than matching the template, which is
precisely why the template keeps winning.

## The test I actually use

Before publishing: if I removed the logo and the brand name, could a
reader tell this came from us rather than any of the other nine?

If not, it is not finished. It is just present.
