The most common headline problem in B2B is not that the writing is bad.
It is that the page has been asked to describe the product, and it has
done so accurately.

These are illustrative examples, not any real brand's copy.

---

## Before

> **AI-powered workflow automation with real-time collaboration**

Everything in that sentence is true and none of it is a reason. It
describes the box, not what happens after you open it.

---

## Fix 1 — Name the thing it removes

> **Stop rebuilding the same handover twice a week**

People do not buy automation. They buy the disappearance of a specific
annoying task. If you can name that task precisely enough that the reader
recognises their own week in it, you have done most of the work.

**Why it works:** specificity is the proof. A reader assumes anyone can
claim "automation". Very few would know to name their exact recurring
irritation.

---

## Fix 2 — Put the changed state in the headline

> **Your team's handovers, finished before the meeting starts**

Same idea, framed as the after rather than the before. Useful when the
pain is mild — nobody is desperate, they are just mildly tired of it, and
a rescue narrative would be overselling.

**Why it works:** it is a picture rather than a promise, and pictures are
harder to disbelieve.

---

## Fix 3 — Lead with the objection

> **Yes, another tool. This one replaces three of them.**

Riskier, and only correct when the audience is genuinely saturated. It
names the thing the reader is already thinking, which buys you the next
five seconds.

**Why it works:** the objection is going to be raised whether or not you
raise it. Doing it first means you get to frame it.

---

## The reusable bit

The test is not "is this headline punchy". It is: **could a competitor
put their logo on this sentence without changing a word?**

For the original, obviously yes. For all three rewrites, no — because
each one commits to something. That is the whole difference.
