## 01 — The brief

Content for SaaS and enterprise B2B clients: long-form SEO articles,
social copy, and the research to support both. Published via Wix, at a
pace that needed a system rather than heroics.

## 02 — The context

Freelance content work usually arrives as a volume request. Thirty blogs.
A month of social. The brief is a quantity.

Underneath the quantity was the more common B2B problem: the clients
could describe their product accurately and still not explain why anyone
should care. Features were clear. The buyer was fuzzy. Which means every
piece of content has to re-derive its own angle from scratch, and thirty
pieces produced that way will contradict each other by about piece
eleven.

## 03 — The observation

The SERP research kept showing the same thing. For most of the target
keywords, the top results were near-identical — same structure, same
subheadings, same three points in the same order. The category had
converged on one shape of answer.

Meanwhile the keyword difficulty and search volume numbers pointed at
something slightly different from where the clients wanted to publish:
the terms with realistic difficulty were narrower and more specific than
the broad category terms everyone was fighting over.

## 04 — The insight

If everyone ranking for a term has written the same article, then
matching that article is the one guaranteed way not to be chosen. Parity
is not a ranking strategy, it is a tie.

And the narrower terms were not a consolation prize. Somebody searching a
specific, awkward, long phrasing is further along in the decision than
somebody searching the category name — so the traffic is smaller and
considerably more useful.

## 05 — The strategy

- **Positioning before production.** Buyer personas, value propositions
  and a positioning line for each client, agreed before the content
  calendar was filled. Everything downstream referenced it.
- **Target realistic terms and go deeper on them** rather than
  competing head-on for category keywords with a new domain.
- **Benefit-led rather than feature-led copy**, which is only possible
  once you have decided who the benefit is for.
- **Platform-specific social**, written per channel with its own hooks
  and CTAs, instead of one post reformatted four ways.
- **AI-assisted workflows for the repeatable parts** — research
  synthesis, outline variations, first drafts of formulaic sections.
  Angle, judgement and final copy stayed manual, because that is the
  part clients are actually paying for.

## 06 — The work

- **30+ long-form B2B blogs**, each preceded by keyword research, SERP
  analysis, and a check on keyword difficulty and search volume
- **60+ social posts** with platform-specific copy, hooks and CTAs
- **Positioning, value propositions and buyer personas** for SaaS and
  enterprise narratives
- **On-page structure and publishing** via Wix
- **AI-assisted content workflows** for research and drafting support

## 07 — The result

30+ long-form blogs and 60+ social posts shipped across the engagement,
on a consistent messaging spine rather than thirty separate improvisations.

I am deliberately not attaching a traffic number to this one. On
freelance engagements I did not own the analytics, and I am not going to
claim a result I could not verify. What I can say is that the output was
consistent, research-led, and did not need rewriting to agree with itself.

## 08 — What I learned

That the fastest way to make a large content order go badly is to start
writing it. The two days spent on personas and value propositions saved
considerably more than two days of rework later.

And that AI in a content workflow is genuinely useful right up to the
point where taste is required, at which point it is a very fast way to
produce forty versions of something mediocre. Knowing where that line
sits is most of the skill.
