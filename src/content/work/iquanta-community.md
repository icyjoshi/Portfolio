## 01 — The brief

Manage a 378K+ member Facebook community of MBA aspirants. Keep it
active, keep it useful, and convert the people in it who were ready to
be converted — with a team of five interns and a query volume that ran
into five figures.

## 02 — The context

MBA aspirants are a particular audience. The stakes feel enormous, the
selection process is opaque, timelines are public and competitive, and
almost everyone in the community is comparing themselves to everyone
else in it. People are online late. People ask the same question
repeatedly because they are hoping for a different answer.

A community that size is not really one community either. It contains
people who have not started preparing, people mid-preparation, and
people waiting on results — three completely different emotional states
sharing one feed.

## 03 — The observation

18,000 queries is usually treated as a support workload. Something to get
through.

But sitting inside that volume, the repetition itself was the finding.
The same anxieties surfaced in slightly different wording over and over,
and the wording clustered by where somebody was in their journey. People
early in preparation asked about *feasibility* — can someone like me do
this. People mid-preparation asked about *method*. People near the end
asked about *comparison*.

Very few of those questions were really requests for information. Most
were requests for reassurance wearing an information question's clothing.

## 04 — The insight

If the questions cluster by stage, then the community is not an audience
to broadcast at — it is a live, continuously updating map of where
people are stuck.

And if most questions are reassurance in disguise, then answering only
the literal question is the reason so many conversations went nowhere.
The ones that converted were the ones where somebody addressed the thing
underneath it.

## 05 — The strategy

- **Treat the query inbox as research, not as a queue.** Recurring
  questions became the content calendar, so the community answered its
  own most common anxieties before people had to ask.
- **Interactive formats over announcements.** Content that invited a
  response gave us more signal and more reach than content that
  broadcast, in an audience that was already highly participatory.
- **Qualify properly rather than chase volume.** Of the total query
  load, a much smaller set were genuinely ready to convert. Those got
  real attention instead of a template.
- **Divide the work by stage across the team of five**, so responses
  matched where the person actually was.

## 06 — The work

- Day-to-day management of a 378K+ member community
- Interactive content formats built around the recurring query themes
- Audience analysis of the MBA-aspirant segment, drawn from what people
  were actually asking
- Query handling and routing across a team of 5 interns
- Lead qualification and conversion on the queries that warranted it

## 07 — The result

**18,000+ user queries handled** across the team.

**78% lead conversion — 91 of 117 user queries.** That is the number I am
most attached to, precisely because it is small. It is a qualified set,
not a vanity total, and the ratio holds up because the qualification
happened before the count.

## 08 — What I learned

That community management is audience research that happens to also be a
job. I have never had a cleaner view of what an audience is actually
worried about than I had reading those queries, and no survey would have
produced it.

Also: a 78% conversion rate on 117 queries is a more honest thing to put
on a portfolio than a bigger percentage on a vaguer denominator. I would
rather show the denominator.
