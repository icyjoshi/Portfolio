## 01 — The brief

Primary research for Smile Train: collect field data, structure it, map
it, and document the process so it could be repeated by someone else.

## 02 — The context

Two months, a specific data need, and a collection process that had to
survive contact with the field — different people, different locations,
inconsistent conditions.

## 03 — The observation

The difficulty in primary research is almost never the analysis. It is
that data collected slightly differently by five people is not one
dataset, it is five small ones that cannot be compared.

## 04 — The insight

Which means the structure has to be decided before collection starts, not
cleaned up afterwards. A tool like KoboToolbox is useful precisely
because it forces that decision early — the form is the methodology.

## 05 — The strategy

Standardise collection first, analyse second, and write the SOP from
what actually caused friction in the field rather than from what the
process was supposed to look like on paper.

## 06 — The work

- Primary research data collection using **KoboToolbox**
- **Geographic mapping** of the collected data
- **Excel** analysis and structuring
- **SOP development and process documentation** for repeatability

## 07 — The result

A documented, repeatable collection and analysis process. This was a
short engagement and I am not going to inflate it — the useful output was
the SOP, and the useful lesson was structural.

## 08 — What I learned

That research design is mostly an exercise in anticipating how people
will fill in a form differently from how you imagined. I still think
about that when I write a survey, a brief, or a content template.
