Most marketing advice arrives as a conclusion. Lead with value. Know your
audience. Be authentic. All defensible, all completely unusable, because
a conclusion with no observation underneath it cannot be argued with or
applied.

The step everyone skips is the one before the conclusion.

## An observation is not an insight

"Gen Z prefers video" is an observation, and a weak one — it is really a
statistic wearing an observation's clothes. It tells you nothing about
what to do, because it does not tell you *why*.

Here is one from a job. Working on a community of MBA aspirants, the same
questions kept arriving in slightly different wording. That is the
observation: repetition, with variation. On its own it is a support
statistic.

The insight is what comes after "which means". The questions clustered by
where somebody was in their preparation — feasibility early, method in
the middle, comparison at the end. And most of them were not requests for
information at all. They were requests for reassurance, phrased as
information questions because that is a more acceptable thing to ask a
stranger.

*Which means* answering the literal question was the reason so many of
those conversations went nowhere.

That is a thing you can act on. "Gen Z prefers video" is not.

## Why the step gets skipped

Because observations are uncomfortable. They are small, specific, and
often slightly embarrassing to say out loud — you noticed something odd
and you cannot yet prove it matters. It is much safer to open with a
framework.

Frameworks are also faster. A SWOT can be filled in without noticing
anything at all, which is exactly why so many of them read as though
nobody did.

## The version I try to hold myself to

Four steps, in order:

1. **Observe.** Something specific. Preferably something that slightly
   surprised you.
2. **Interpret.** Finish the sentence "…which means…". If you cannot,
   you do not have an insight yet, and you should say so rather than
   dressing it up.
3. **Recommend.** Decide something. A read of a situation that ends
   without a decision is a book report.
4. **Make it.** The recommendation is not the deliverable. The thing is.

The hard step is the second one, and it is the only step that cannot be
outsourced — not to a framework, not to a template, and not to a model
that will happily generate forty confident interpretations of an
observation you have not thought about yet.

## The honest caveat

Sometimes you notice something, follow it, and it turns out to mean
nothing. That is a normal outcome and not a failure of the method.

The failure is noticing nothing and producing a deck anyway.
